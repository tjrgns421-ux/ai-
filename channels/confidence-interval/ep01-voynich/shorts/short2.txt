One argument kept the Voynich manuscript from being a cipher: no medieval cipher looks like this.
In 2025, Michael Greshko built one that does. He calls it the Naibbe cipher.
Take Latin text. Break it into short pieces. Roll a die, draw a playing card, and swap each piece using a table. Everything a fifteenth-century writer had on the desk.
The output copies the manuscript's quirks: symbol frequencies, word lengths, where glyphs sit inside a word.

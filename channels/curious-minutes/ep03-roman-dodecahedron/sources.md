## W. Wikipedia "Roman dodecahedron" (2026-11-13 판, 각 문장의 원 출처는 각주)
- W1 "small hollow object made of copper alloy ... cast into a regular dodecahedral shape with twelve flat pentagonal faces. Each face has a circular hole of varying diameter ... each corner has a protruding knob" ; "rarely show signs of wear and do not have any inscribed numbers or letters"
- W2 "Since the first known example was recorded in 1739, over one hundred such objects have been discovered, dating from the 2nd to the 4th centuries AD. Their purpose or meaning has been long debated but remains unknown."
- W3 "vary in size from 4–11 cm ... holes ... 6–40 mm. The lightest known example weighs 35 g ...; the heaviest 1 kg"
- W4 "cast using the lost wax technique"; "outsides are well-finished and were probably polished"; "no letters or numbers"
- W5 first recorded "presented to the Society of Antiquaries of London on 28 June 1739 ... found in a field in Aston, Hertfordshire"
- W6 "about 130 similar objects have been found in Austria, Belgium, France, Germany, Hungary, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Switzerland, and the United Kingdom, but not in the Roman heartland in Italy"; "southeasternmost findspot ... Deonica, Serbia"; Slovakia fragments "outside of the boundaries of the Roman empire"
- W7 "In 2023, a dodecahedron in excellent condition was found by amateur archeologists in ... Norton Disney in Lincolnshire, UK, bringing the total to 33 ... Roman Britain"; "on display at the Lincoln Museum ... until October 2024"
- W8 context "commonly military or funerary; other discoveries have been in baths, a theatre, a coin hoard, and on a riverbed"; "mostly made in Gaul"
- W9 "more than fifty possible explanations have been published"; "no mention of dodecahedra has been found in contemporary accounts or pictures"
- W10 survey instrument "questioned, as there are no markings"; knitting: "earliest known reference to spool knitting is from 1535"; "neither explain the use of bronze, nor the ... icosahedron"; child's toy
- W11 fortune-telling; coin hoards → "checking that coins fit a certain diameter"; test of a metalsmith's skill
- W12 Roman icosahedron "near Arloff in Germany ... Rheinisches Landesmuseum in Bonn"; "solid silver Roman dodecahedron was discovered in Geneva in 1982"
- W13 gold dodecahedra in Southeast Asia (Óc Eo, Vietnam; Malleret)

## G. Google Arts & Culture, "Can You Decode the Roman Dodecahedron?" (Corbridge Roman Museum object)
- G1 "from Hadrian's Wall in England to Avenches in Switzerland and even as far as eastern Hungary"; "There are no ancient writings or images of these objects, and their use is a mystery."
- G2 "116 of these objects have been discovered"
- G3 "evidently costly and difficult objects to make"
- G4 found in "Roman military camps, public baths, temples, theatres, and tombs ... often in areas where Celtic tribes interacted with Romans"
- G5 dice/fortune telling: "experiments with reproductions show they are difficult to roll"
- G6 "Traces of wax have been found on some examples" → candle holders
- G7 surveying: "with no standardisation, they wouldn't be much use"
- G8 sundial: "the sun would align with two of the holes ... largely found in northern Europe, where the growing season is short"
- G9 knitting: proposed "by a knitting hobbyist"; "no evidence for knitting until at least 1,000 years after"

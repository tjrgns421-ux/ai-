More than a hundred of these bronze objects have been found across the old Roman Empire, from Britain to Hungary. They were costly to make, carefully finished, and found in camps, baths, temples and tombs. Yet no Roman ever described one. So what was the Roman dodecahedron for?
We look at what we actually know, test the five most famous theories, and see where a 2025 study in The Antiquaries Journal points.

The photos show real dodecahedrons in museums. The 3D scenes are illustrations.

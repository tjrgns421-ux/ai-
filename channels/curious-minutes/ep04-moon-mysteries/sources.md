## 1. Mystery hut (SOLVED)
- H1 Live Science (Stephanie Pappas, 2022-01-07): "nicknamed 'jade rabbit' by the Yutu 2 team, which announced its rover's closer inspection of the object on Friday (Jan. 7)"; "Yutu, also translates to 'jade rabbit'"; first appeared "in December, when it looked like a cube-shaped blur on the horizon"; Our Space "joked that it might be the hut of alien pioneers"; "After a month-long traverse"; "the rock is much smaller than it appeared to be from afar. It's also much rounder"; Andrew Jones: "The Moon's surface is 38 million square kilometres of rocks"
- H2 Space.com (Leonard David): Stooke: "It's just a rock on a crater rim"; "But it won't look like a hut."
## 2. The "gel" (SOLVED, analysis not definitive)
- G1 Space.com: found "in July 2019, during lunar day 8"; Our Space used "胶状物" = "gel-like"; Gou Sheng et al., Earth and Planetary Science Letters: "a dark greenish and glistening impact melt breccia, measuring 20 inches by 6 inches (52 by 16 centimeters)"; resembles Apollo samples 15466 and 70019; "The results are not definitive"
- G2 Live Science: "It turned out to be melted-together rock caused by a long-ago meteorite impact, a 2020 study found."
## 3. The Moon "rang" (explained)
- B1 Wikipedia, Apollo 12 Passive Seismic Experiment: LM ascent stage "hit the lunar surface at 6,048 km/h and created an estimated 9 meter wide crater"; "the Moon vibrating for over 55 minutes"; signals "totally unlike any received before"
- B2 Wikipedia, Moonquake: shaking "can last for up to an hour, due to fewer attenuating factors to damp seismic vibrations"
## 4. The Moon is rusting (BEST GUESS)
- R1 NASA/JPL "The Moon Is Rusting, and Researchers Want to Know Why" (2020): hematite "normally requires oxygen and water"; Science Advances paper; Chandrayaan-1 Moon Mineralogy Mapper (built by JPL); Shuai Li; Fraeman: "At first, I totally didn't believe it."; solar wind hydrogen "makes it harder for hematite to form"; Earth's magnetotail carries oxygen (Kaguya, 2007); "more hematite on the Moon's Earth-facing near side than on its far side"; magnetotail "blocks over 99% of the solar wind ... whenever it's in the full Moon phase"; "why smaller quantities of hematite are also forming on the far side ... where the Earth's oxygen shouldn't be able to reach it"
## 5. Lunar swirls (BEST GUESS)
- S1 NASA Science, "Lunar Swirl Reiner Gamma": "bright, often sinuous features ... unique to the Moon"; "visible from a backyard telescope"; "tendrils that extend for several hundred kilometers"; "recent modeling and spacecraft data shed light on the origin of these swirls – crustal magnetism"; "Think of lunar swirls as the Moon's tan lines"
## 6. Two-faced Moon (STILL UNKNOWN / debated)
- F1 Wikipedia, Far side of the Moon: "Only 1% of the surface of the far side is covered by maria, compared to 31.2% on the near side"; first photographed by Luna 3, 7 Oct 1959; Chang'e 4 first far-side landing 3 Jan 2019; Chang'e 6 first far-side samples, returned 25 June 2024; explanations: heat-producing elements on the near side; heat from Earth when the Moon formed → thicker far-side crust

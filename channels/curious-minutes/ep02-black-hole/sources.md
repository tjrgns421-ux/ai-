## A. NASA Goddard (2024-05-06) "New NASA Black Hole Visualization Takes Viewers Beyond the Brink" (Francis Reddy)
https://science.nasa.gov/supermassive-black-holes/new-nasa-black-hole-visualization-takes-viewers-beyond-the-brink/
- A1 Schnittman: "two different scenarios, one where a camera — a stand-in for a daring astronaut — just misses the event horizon and slingshots back out, and one where it crosses the boundary"
- A2 Discover supercomputer, "about 10 terabytes of data", "about 5 days running on just 0.3% of Discover's 129,000 processors", "more than a decade on a typical laptop"
- A3 "a supermassive black hole with 4.3 million times the mass of our Sun, equivalent to the monster located at the center of our Milky Way galaxy"
- A4 Schnittman: "If you have the choice, you want to fall into a supermassive black hole." "Stellar-mass black holes, which contain up to about 30 solar masses, possess much smaller event horizons and stronger tidal forces, which can rip apart approaching objects before they get to the horizon."
- A5 "the gravitational pull on the end of an object nearer the black hole is much stronger than that on the other end. Infalling objects stretch out like noodles, a process astrophysicists call spaghettification."
- A6 "event horizon spans about 16 million miles (25 million kilometers), or about 17% of the distance from Earth to the Sun"
- A7 "the glow from the accretion disk and background stars becomes amplified ... Their light appears brighter and whiter when looking into the direction of travel."
- A8 "begin with the camera located nearly 400 million miles (640 million kilometers) away"
- A9 "the camera takes about 3 hours to fall to the event horizon, executing almost two complete 30-minute orbits along the way"
- A10 "to anyone observing from afar, it would never quite get there ... the image of the camera would slow and then seem to freeze just shy of it. This is why astronomers originally referred to black holes as 'frozen stars.'"
- A11 "At the event horizon, even space-time itself flows inward at the speed of light"; singularity "where the laws of physics as we know them cease to operate"
- A12 Schnittman: "Once the camera crosses the horizon, its destruction by spaghettification is just 12.8 seconds away." "only 79,500 miles (128,000 kilometers) to the singularity"
- A13 "6-hour round trip ... she'd return 36 minutes younger than her colleagues. That's because time passes more slowly near a strong gravitational source and when moving near the speed of light."
- A14 Schnittman: "If the black hole were rapidly rotating, like the one shown in the 2014 movie 'Interstellar,' she would return many years younger than her shipmates."

## B. NASA SVS 14576 explainer captions / SVS 14585 (2024-05-06, Schnittman & Powell)
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/14576 · https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/14585
- B1 "The thin inner circle is called the photon ring. It's an image produced by light that has orbited the black hole one or more times before escaping."
- B2 "For the camera, time slows compared to that of a faraway observer."
- B3 "Light from the outside universe still shines in, but can never leave."
- B4 "The camera is destroyed. Microseconds later, it reaches the singularity."
- B5 "Due to the camera's speed, the entire sky appears to shift progressively forward. The sky is shrinking before our eyes."
- B6 (14585) "moving inward at 19% the speed of light — nearly 127 million mph (205 million kph)" at "about 70 million miles (113 million kilometers) from the black hole's event horizon"

## C. NASA Science, Black holes: Anatomy / Types
https://science.nasa.gov/universe/black-holes/anatomy/ · https://science.nasa.gov/universe/black-holes/types/
- C1 event horizon: "Inside this boundary, the velocity needed to escape the black hole exceeds the speed of light ... So whatever passes into the event horizon is doomed to stay inside it – even light."
- C2 accretion disk: "The gas settles into a hot, bright, rapidly spinning disk."
- C3 singularity: "may be either a physical structure or a purely mathematical one, but right now astronomers don't know which is true."
- C4 stellar-mass: star "around 20 times the Sun's mass or more ... core collapses into a stellar-mass black hole"; "as many as 100 million in our galaxy alone"
- C5 "Almost every large galaxy, including our Milky Way, has a supermassive black hole at its center."
- C6 Sagittarius A* "is 4 million times the mass of the Sun"; Holmberg 15A "at least 40 billion solar masses"

## E. ESO eso2208 (2022-05-12) first image of Sagittarius A* (Event Horizon Telescope)
https://www.eso.org/public/news/eso2208-eht-mw/
- E1 "four million times more massive than our Sun"; "about 27 000 light-years away"; "about the same size in the sky as a doughnut on the Moon"
- E2 "linked together eight existing radio observatories across the planet to form a single 'Earth-sized' virtual telescope"; observed "in 2017"
- E3 "Although we cannot see the black hole itself, because it is completely dark, glowing gas around it reveals ... a dark central region (called a shadow) surrounded by a bright ring-like structure."

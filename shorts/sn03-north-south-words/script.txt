In North Korea, there's a law against sounding South Korean.
It was adopted in January 2023. Sixty-five articles. And according to reports, the punishment can go up to years in prison. Some outlets say even death.
Choose your words carefully. Literally.
But wait. It's the same language. Same alphabet, same grammar. Until 1945, it was one country.
So, three questions. How did one language split in two? How different is it really? And why would a government ban an accent?
First, the split. After 1945, the peninsula was divided, and each side picked its own standard.
The South built its standard on the speech of Seoul.
In 1966, the North made its own official. It would be based on Pyongyang speech, and it got a name: munhwaeo, the "cultured language".
And the North started cleaning house. Foreign loanwords, out. Native Korean words, in.
Shampoo became "hair water soap". Juice became "sweet water".

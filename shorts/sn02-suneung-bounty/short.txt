South Korea will pay up to seventy-five thousand dollars for one tip about cheating on one exam.
And a cheater could be banned from that exam for ten years.
The exam is the Suneung, Korea's college entrance test. More than five hundred fifty thousand people applied this year.
Planes stop during the English listening section, because your university can shape your whole career.
So why the bounty? Days before the exam, the top scorer on Korea's tax accountant exam confessed he cheated, using a hidden camera and generative AI.
So today, proctors checked every pair of glasses, and covered shirt buttons with stickers.
But here's the catch. Most of the new penalties are still a plan.
The reward still needs new rules. The ten-year ban needs a new law, and the ruling party says it will fast-track one.
From November twentieth, a task force led by the prime minister will work with AI companies to look for cheaters.
One Reddit user put it this way: suddenly, everyone is a proctor now.
